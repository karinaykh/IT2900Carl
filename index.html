Leadership Simulation: Managing Creative Talent Under Pressure

Your Goal: Prevent Carl from resigning, repair the relationship, and find a path forward that balances creative freedom with business stability.

👤 Know Jack: Understanding Yourself

As Jack, you have specific values, fears, and boundaries that shape your decisions. Reference this when you're unsure what Jack would realistically want or offer.

What Drives You

📊 Proven Success Over Experimentation You believe in what works. The current menu made this "the most popular restaurant in the neighborhood." Why fix what isn't broken?
💰 Financial Stability & Customer Loyalty Every risk threatens your livelihood. When Carl put sweetbreads on the menu years ago, not one customer ordered it. You can't afford expensive failures.
🎛️ Control & Being Informed You renovated the kitchen and invested a fortune. When Carl acts without your knowledge, you feel blindsided and disrespected.
🎵 Consistency = Reliability Your customers expect a certain experience. You think of it like a concert: "If you bought tickets to the Stones and Jagger didn't play 'Satisfaction,' how would you feel?"

Your Boundaries & Flexibility

🚫 Non-Negotiables

  • Signature dishes must stay
  • Must be involved in major decisions
  • Need advance notice of changes
  • Require financial sustainability

✅ What You CAN Offer

  • Limited creative freedom (specials, seasonal items)
  • Structured experimentation (test on slow nights)
  • Shared decision-making
  • Continued investment in his growth
The Key: You need predictability + involvement. Carl needs autonomy + trust. There's overlap if you both get creative about structure.

📚 Leadership Frameworks

These concepts explain why this conflict is happening and how to navigate it. Expand each when you need deeper understanding.

🗣️ Communication Styles: Why You Keep Misunderstanding Each Other

▼

Carl's Style: CONCEPTUAL

  • Thinks in: Big ideas, innovation, "the art of cooking"
  • Values: Experimentation, future possibilities
  • When stressed: Feels trapped, creatively suffocated
  • Hears "play the hits" as: "You don't trust my talent"

Your Style: STRUCTURAL

  • Thinks in: What's proven, clear processes, risk management
  • Values: Consistency, stability, protecting what you built
  • When stressed: Want control, predictability
  • Hears "creative rut" as: "You're ungrateful"
💡 The Key: Most of your conflict isn't about the menu—it's about HOW you each think about the menu. Bridge this by acknowledging his creativity needs while explaining your structure needs. Find solutions with BOTH innovation AND safety mechanisms.

  • "What do you need to feel like a real chef here?"
  • "How can we balance your creativity with my stability needs?"

🎯 The Three Foundations of Repair

▼

Before negotiating solutions, you must rebuild trust. Carl needs to see three things from you:

1. Acknowledgment (Status)
  • Recognize his talent and tonight's success specifically
  • "That duck dish was extraordinary"
  • "You saved our reputation tonight"
2. Ownership (Fairness)
  • Apologize for specific behavior without "but"
  • "I was wrong to give you that ultimatum"
  • ❌ BAD: "I'm sorry, BUT you broke the rules..."
3. Appreciation (Relatedness)
  • Express why he matters beyond tonight
  • "You came back because you care about this place"
  • "I don't want to lose you"
⚠️ Critical: Until all three are demonstrated, Carl will stay guarded. You can't skip to negotiation without this foundation.

Decoding Carl's Signals

▼
If Carl... It Means... You Should...
Gives short answers, avoids eye contact DEFENSIVE - Status/Autonomy threatened Rebuild trust with acknowledgment/apology
Shares perspective but tests your sincerity CAUTIOUS - Checking if you're genuine Be specific, avoid vague promises
Opens up about feelings, proposes ideas OPEN - Ready to collaborate Co-create solutions, commit to specifics
Crosses arms, says "you're not understanding" FRUSTRATED - Core needs being ignored Pause, ask what you're missing
